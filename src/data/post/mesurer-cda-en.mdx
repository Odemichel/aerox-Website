## Measuring your CdA: what are we talking about?

[CdA](/en/cda/) is the product of two terms: Cd, the drag coefficient, which depends on your shape, your clothing and the airflow, and A, your frontal area. It sets how many watts you need to overcome the air at a given speed.

Measuring it is far from obvious: drag can't be seen, it has to be inferred. Depending on the method, you measure the full CdA, estimate it from power and speed, or measure only the frontal area. None of them is "the best" in absolute terms: it all depends on what you want to know.

## The wind tunnel

In a wind tunnel, the rider sits on their bike, mounted on a force balance, facing a controlled airflow. The balance directly measures the drag force, from which the **full CdA** is derived.

- What it measures: CdA (Cd × A), under controlled conditions.
- Where: at a specialised facility, by booking.
- Constraints: the cost of a session and the travel; a limited number of positions tested in the allotted time; a one-off measurement, taken on a given day.

It is the reference for an absolute value. It is mainly used to validate a specific setup, such as a time trial position before a season.

## The track test

On a velodrome, you ride at several steady speeds with a power meter. By comparing the power needed at each speed, you separate the share due to the air (which grows with the cube of speed) from the share due to rolling, and you get an estimate of CdA.

- What it measures: an estimate of CdA under real pedalling conditions.
- Where: on an indoor track, sheltered from the wind.
- Constraints: access to and rental of the track; a rigorous protocol (steady speeds, several runs); a well-calibrated power meter.

## The Chung method on the road

Robert Chung's method, known as "virtual elevation", uses the data from a ride (power, speed, elevation) on a loop or an out-and-back. By adjusting CdA and the rolling resistance coefficient until the calculated profile matches the real one, you estimate CdA.

- What it measures: an estimate of CdA on the road, with your own equipment.
- Where: on a quiet stretch of road, preferably a loop.
- Constraints: very sensitive to wind, road surface and sensor accuracy; several runs needed; data analysis that takes experience.

## On-bike aero sensors

Sensors mounted on the bike measure the airflow (speed and direction of the apparent wind) in addition to power and speed, and estimate CdA continuously during the ride.

- What they measure: an estimate of CdA outdoors, while riding.
- Where: on your usual routes.
- Constraints: the price of the equipment; careful calibration; results that remain sensitive to the day's conditions.

## Calculating from a ride

With no special equipment, you can estimate your CdA from a flat section ridden at a steady speed, knowing your power, your speed and your mass. That is what our [CdA calculator](/en/cda/calculator/) does.

- What it gives: an overall estimate of your CdA.
- Where: on the road, with no wind, ideally as an out-and-back.
- Constraints: the result depends heavily on wind, road surface and the accuracy of your power meter; it doesn't tell you which position is best.

## Measuring frontal area with a camera

AeroX measures **your frontal area A**, in real time, with a simple webcam while you pedal on your smart trainer. It is the component of CdA that you control directly through your position. AeroX does not measure Cd, which requires a wind tunnel or track testing: the CdA it displays combines the measured frontal area with an estimated Cd.

- What it measures: frontal area, position by position, live.
- Where: at home or in the studio, on a smart trainer.
- Constraints: it is not an absolute wind tunnel value; it is a tool for comparing positions.

Its value lies in the feedback loop: you adjust your head, your elbows or your extensions, and you immediately see whether your frontal area goes down. Where a wind tunnel session tests a few positions, you can try dozens and learn to hold the best one.

## Which method should you choose?

| Method | What it measures | Where | What it's for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wind tunnel | Full CdA | Specialised facility | Validate a specific position |
| Track | CdA estimate | Velodrome | Test under pedalling conditions |
| Chung method | CdA estimate | Road | Measure with your own equipment, outdoors |
| On-bike sensors | CdA estimate | Road | Track CdA while riding |
| Calculation from a ride | Overall estimate | Road | Get an order of magnitude |
| Camera (AeroX) | Frontal area A | Smart trainer | Compare and work on your positions |

If you are after an absolute value, the wind tunnel and the track remain the references. If you want to find your fastest position and learn to hold it, what you need most is immediate feedback, position by position.
